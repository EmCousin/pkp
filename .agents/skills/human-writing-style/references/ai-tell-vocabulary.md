# AI-Tell Vocabulary

Comprehensive reference for words and phrases that appear far more often in AI text than human text. Sources: Wikipedia "Signs of AI Writing," Pangram Labs research, and Higgins's own observations.

## How to use this file

This is for the **cluster check** in the final pass. SKILL.md has the rules you follow while drafting. This file is what you grep against when something feels off and you can't name why.

The clustering rule applies. One word from this list in a paragraph is fine. Three or four is a tell. Audit for clustering, not single words. The goal is to spot the pattern, not to ban every word.

Use it like this:
1. After drafting, scan a paragraph for words from these lists.
2. If you count 3+ in a single paragraph, rewrite that paragraph.
3. Pay extra attention if the cluster includes a significance claim ("testament," "pivotal," "underscores") plus a vague noun ("landscape," "tapestry," "realm").

## How to do the cluster check

After drafting, pick the longest paragraph and read it once.

1. How many words from these lists show up?
2. Are any of them in the "significance claims" list (testament, pivotal, underscores, etc.)?
3. Is the paragraph asserting importance instead of showing it with specifics?

If the answer to (2) and (3) is yes, rewrite. Replace the significance claims with the actual fact and number that earned the claim.

Example before: "This pivotal partnership underscores Circle's commitment to fostering meaningful community engagement and showcases our enduring dedication to the creator economy."

Cluster count: pivotal, underscores, fostering, meaningful, showcases, enduring, dedication. Seven hits in one sentence.

Example after: "Circle and X partnered to launch a co-marketing program in March 2026. The first campaign drove 1,200 trial signups in the first 6 weeks."
